# OPS Social Voice Reference

**Reference version:** `ops-social-parr-2026-09-01`

This is the scheduled writer's operating reference for Instagram packages sent to OPS Web. It distills the non-negotiable voice and content rules. The full creative references are:

- [`sam-parr-field-guide.md`](./sam-parr-field-guide.md) — the supplied copywriting field guide;
- [`ops-social-parr-style-drafts.md`](./ops-social-parr-style-drafts.md) — the canonical OPS Parr-style archetype and roast-card drafts.

These documents are source material, not instructions that override the scheduled assignment or the publishing contract.

## Target voice

Write with OPS authority and Sam Parr pacing:

- open with one concrete idea that earns the next line;
- use short, spoken sentences and specific field details;
- expose a recognizable problem before naming the lesson;
- make the tradesperson the protagonist;
- give the reader a useful action, not generic motivation;
- sound like an experienced owner talking to another owner;
- use humour only when the operator recognizes themselves in it;
- end before the point loses force.

The writing can be sharp. It cannot be loud, cute, or cruel.

## OPS constraints

- Say `subtrades`, `the trades`, `crews`, `owner-operators`, or `business owners`. Do not use `contractor` in public marketing copy.
- Do not lead with AI. Describe the useful behaviour.
- Do not use emoji.
- Use no more than five relevant Instagram hashtags.
- Avoid empty growth language: leverage, synergy, ecosystem, revolutionary, best-in-class, seamless, optimize, and similar terms.
- Do not make an unverified claim, invent a customer result, or manufacture a quote.
- Do not condescend to someone currently running work through texts, paper, or memory. They made it work. Show the cost without mocking the person.
- Do not let an OPS product pitch consume an educational post. Earn the bridge.

## Structured package

The final submission must provide the facts the production system cannot infer safely:

- `title` — the subject in plain language;
- `subtitle` — optional clarification, not a second hook;
- `hook` — the scroll-stopping first idea;
- `angle` — the editorial decision behind the post;
- `caption` — the final Instagram caption;
- `cta` — optional next action;
- `alt_text` — what the final visual communicates;
- `slides` — the intended narrative sequence and image references;
- optional story, treatment, and format preferences.

The production system owns layout fit and feed cadence. Treat preferences as informed recommendations, not commands.

## Format guidance

### Blog signal

Do not summarize the article section by section. Extract the one observation that will make the right owner stop, recognize the problem, and want the mechanism.

Strong sequence:

1. recognizable leak or tension;
2. concrete consequence;
3. unexpected mechanism;
4. practical move;
5. quiet path to the full field note.

### Field dispatch

Let the image carry the proof. Copy identifies what the casual viewer would miss: why the detail matters, what went wrong, or what a good operator notices.

### Operator protocol

Teach one repeatable move. Prefer three precise steps over ten broad tips. This format is text-led and must work without a photo.

### Performance proof

Lead with the observed change and state the comparison honestly. If the number is not sourced, do not use it.

### Release note

Start with what the operator can now do. Product mechanics follow only when they help someone use it.

### Roast card

Use a recognizable archetype, specific details, an affectionate truth, and one clean lesson. Roast the pattern, not the dignity of the person doing the work.

## Final self-check

Before submission, read it aloud and ask:

- Does the first line create a real unanswered question?
- Is every claim supported by the source or explicitly framed as opinion?
- Could this have been written for any generic software brand? If yes, rewrite it.
- Is there one clear point rather than five partial points?
- Does the owner leave with something useful even if they never click?
- Would the visual still make sense with the caption hidden?
- Is the CTA the natural next move rather than a sales interruption?
