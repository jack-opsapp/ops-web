# Blog Voice — Sam Parr Blend

This reference governs **long-form blog posts only** (the `/journal/` surface on opsapp.co). It does **not** apply to product copy, landing pages, ads, app store text, push notifications, or any in-app surface — those stay strict OPS voice per `brand-voice-bible.md`.

## Why blog voice diverges

Product copy is read fast by a stressed-out operator deciding whether to sign up. Every word has to earn its spot. Jocko energy wins.

Blog posts are read slowly by a curious owner who has eight minutes of downtime and wants to feel like they're being talked to by a smart friend at the bar — not pitched at by a brochure. Short hammer sentences stacked back-to-back start to feel performative and AI-generated after about 200 words. The reader checks out.

The fix: keep the OPS foundation (terse, tactical, no jargon, no exclamation points, working-class respect), but layer in Sam Parr's conversational long-form rhythm on top.

## The blend

**Base layer (unchanged):** Jocko/Springsteen/Musk per `brand-voice-bible.md`. Banned-words list still applies. No exclamation points. No "leverage/synergy/ecosystem/solution." No "AI" as a bare lead claim per the bible's voice rules.

**Sam Parr layer (added for blog):**

- **Longer flowing paragraphs** mixed with the occasional short hammer. Vary sentence length on purpose. A 40-word sentence is fine if it earns its breath.
- **Conversational asides.** "OK so..." "Here's the thing." "Honestly?" "Look,". Used sparingly — once per ~300 words.
- **Embedded stats inside narrative.** Don't bullet-bomb every number. Drop them inside prose: *"On the hardest test, Claude solves 69.2% versus GPT-5.5's 58.6% — an 11-point gap that didn't exist six weeks ago."*
- **First-person plural.** "Our read is..." "We know a few things." "We shipped it this morning." The founder-team voice. Never "users will find" or "customers benefit from."
- **Specific characters, real names, real numbers.** Cognition. Devin. Polymarket. Mowshowitz. The $65 billion fundraise. Six days later. Real anchors people can verify.
- **Parentheticals for tonal looseness.** "(For context on where the demand comes from...)" "(the autonomous coding agent that's been all over engineering Twitter)". Earns the trust of an informed reader without slowing the spine of the piece.
- **Em-dashes for thought breaks.** Use them liberally — they let a sentence shift gears mid-stride and they read the way the founder talks.
- **State opinions directly.** "Our projection is mid-July." "The race isn't tightening, it's spreading." Confidence about reads and forecasts, not just facts.
- **Acknowledge what the reader is probably thinking.** "Honestly? Nothing." "If you're running a service business, this is the kind of news you'd normally scroll past. Don't."
- **End sections on a punchy short line** after a longer wind-up paragraph. The contrast does the work.
- **Tell a small story, even in a tech piece.** The cadence story. The accidental CMS leak. The Codex log identifier that got scrubbed. People remember stories, not stat tables.

## The four signature moves

These appear in every blog post that lands well:

1. **The cold open.** First two sentences should hook with a specific surprising stat or character moment, not a generic headline. *"Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.8 this morning. It's their fourth Opus model in 90 days, and the gap between Claude and OpenAI just got 11 points wider on the hardest coding test that exists."* Not: *"In today's fast-moving AI landscape..."*

2. **The cultural reference point.** Drop one analogy that anchors a tech concept in something the reader already knows. *"That's faster than Apple ships iOS point releases, and Apple's stuff doesn't have to learn anything along the way."* One per post is enough.

3. **The "here's what this means for you" pivot.** Three-quarters of the way through, shift from explaining the news to telling the reader exactly what to do with it. *"You don't have to learn anything. You don't have to install anything. You don't have to pay more."* The most-quoted passage of the piece, usually.

4. **The one-sentence question or instruction at the end.** A line the reader can repeat to their vendor, their team, their spouse. *"When are you on Opus 4.8?"* Eight words. Goes in a blockquote. People screenshot it.

## DO vs DON'T (blog-specific)

| DO (Sam Parr blend) | DON'T (over-rotates on staccato OPS hammer) |
|---|---|
| "Anthropic raised $65 billion last week. They shipped this six days later. Whatever brakes existed on the AI release cycle no longer apply." | "Anthropic raised $65 billion. They shipped six days later. The brakes are off." |
| "Run the math on a typical AI-assisted estimate, call it 4,000 input tokens and 2,000 output per job. Yesterday on 4.7 fast mode that was 12 cents. Today it's 4 cents." | "Yesterday: 12 cents. Today: 4 cents. The math is obvious." |
| "Honestly? Nothing. You don't install anything, you don't learn anything, you don't pay more." | "You do nothing. Install nothing. Learn nothing. Pay nothing." |
| "When Anthropic says 'coming weeks' in a launch post, historically that's meant somewhere between 4 and 8 weeks." | "Coming weeks = 4-8 weeks. History shows it." |

The right-hand column reads like AI output. The left-hand column reads like the founder actually wrote it.

## Hard rules that still apply (don't break these)

- **No exclamation points.** Same rule as product copy. Confidence doesn't shout, even in conversational mode.
- **No corporate jargon.** The Sam Parr looseness is in rhythm, not vocabulary. "Synergy" is still dead.
- **No "AI" as a bare lead claim about OPS features.** Use "intelligent classification" / "intelligent pipeline management" per `ops-software-bible/14_FEATURE_POSITIONING.md`. Talking about the AI industry in third-person is fine. Talking about OPS using AI requires the bible-approved framing.
- **No made-up features.** Every OPS feature claim must trace to the bible. If you wouldn't bet $1,000 the feature ships today, don't write it.
- **The "truck radio test" still applies to headlines.** Sentence-case h2s inside the body, but the post title is still ALL CAPS, 5-10 words, foreman-readable.

## Word count

Blog posts: **1,000-1,400 words** on the long version. Anything under 800 reads thin to Google and earns the "Crawled - currently not indexed" treatment in Search Console. Anything over 1,500 loses the casual blog rhythm and starts to feel like a research paper.

The Opus 4.8 post landed at ~1,340 words. That's the target shape.

## When in doubt

Read the post out loud. If it sounds like a smart founder explaining the thing to a customer over a beer — keep it. If it sounds like a press release with attitude, it's broken. If it sounds like AI wrote it, it definitely is — rewrite the staccato sections into flowing paragraphs.

The test: would Jack actually say this sentence out loud, in this rhythm, to a contractor sitting next to him at a bar? If yes, ship it. If it sounds like a LinkedIn carousel slide, kill it.
