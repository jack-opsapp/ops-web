## Topic

Getting paid is decided at signing: a written payment schedule tied to finished work and signed change orders, so a job never ends in a teardown.

Category: `money-and-margins`

## The reader

An owner-operator running a small residential crew (fences, decks, roofs, remodels) who has finished the work and watched the last payment stall: "I did the job, he's got the fence, and I'm the one chasing him."

## Why now

The comments argue about whether the teardown was justice or a lawsuit. Underneath that argument is what every owner has lived: the last payment is won or lost at signing, not at the last post.

## Fit with the OPS ethos

The brief names getting paid as core OPS ground, and ownership of the paper is discipline, not luck. The founder's ten years in deck and rail is the same backyard, fixed-structure work as fencing, where leverage ends the moment the last board goes up. The one move is concrete:
- a payment schedule tied to finished phases, written before the first hole is dug
- no extra work without a signed change order

One OPS fact can land late and soft: the approved estimate converts to an invoice with deposit, milestones and balance already structured, and the client signs the estimate on their phone.

## Grounding pages

Primary sources come first. In the real run, OPS fetches these, and every quote and number is copied from the fetched page, not from these search notes.

1. **California Contractors State License Board: "Learn About Home Improvement Contracts"** (state regulator). https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/What_Is_A_Contract.aspx
   Would back: the California cap on down payments for home improvement jobs, and the rule that a payment schedule must describe each phase of work and the payment tied to it.
2. **CSLB Industry Bulletin: "Progress Payment Restrictions"** (2022, regulator bulletin). https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2022/Industry_Bulletin_Progress_Payment_Restrictions.pdf
   Would back: progress payments may not run ahead of the work completed and materials delivered, which is the mechanism behind a phase-tied schedule.
3. **Illinois Attorney General: "Home Repair and Construction: Know Your Rights"** (state regulator, the state where the teardown happened). https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/Page-Attachments/Home%20Repair_KnowYourRights.pdf
   Would back: Illinois requires a signed written contract before home repair or remodeling work over $1,000, and what that contract must state.
4. **Illinois Home Repair and Remodeling Act, 815 ILCS 513** (statute text via Justia; prefer the ilga.gov original on fetch). https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-815/act-815-ilcs-513
